    The short answer: 3% to 8% of what you paid

    Korean shelf prices include 10% VAT. That tax is 1/11 of the price — about 9.1% — and that is the ceiling, not what lands in your hand. Refunds are handled by licensed operators (Global Blue, Global Tax Free, CubeRefund, Easy Tax Refund, NICE and others), and each one keeps a handling fee that the National Tax Service has approved. The result is a refund of roughly 3% to 8%, paid in fixed steps by receipt total rather than as a flat percentage.

    Receipts never combine. The minimum, the tiers and the caps all apply per receipt. Whether one big payment beats several smaller ones depends on the tier lines: two ₩30,000 receipts return ₩4,000 together, while a single ₩60,000 receipt returns ₩3,000.

    Why a ₩1,000 extra item can be worth ₩2,000

    Because refunds move in tiers. Spend ₩74,000 and the refund is ₩3,000; spend ₩75,000 and it is ₩5,000. The same jump repeats up the table — ₩124,999 returns ₩7,000, while ₩125,000 returns ₩8,000. If you were going to add one more small item anyway, checking the next tier line first is free money.

    At the airport, step by step

    1. Shop with your passport. Buy at a shop with a “Tax Refund” or “Tax Free” sign and show your passport at the till.
    2. Keep the goods unused. Customs can ask to see them, and used or opened items can lose the refund.
    3. Checked bag? Get the customs confirmation at the counter before the bag goes on the belt. Once it is checked in, the refund is gone.
    4. Carry-on? Go through security first, then use a kiosk in the departure area.
    5. Scan and collect. Passport plus refund receipt at the kiosk; if it flags a check, take the item and receipt to the customs desk. Choose cash or card refund.

    The most expensive mistake: dropping a tax-refund purchase in checked baggage before customs has seen it. Ask at the counter, get the stamp, then check the bag in.

    Who qualifies

    • Foreign tourists staying in Korea for six months or less, with no Korean income.
    • Overseas Koreans who have lived abroad for at least two years and are staying three months or less.
    • Goods bought at a participating shop, worth ₩15,000 or more per receipt, taken out within three months.

    Diplomats stationed in Korea, US Forces Korea personnel, people employed in Korea, airline crew, and purchases made under a company name or through an agent are outside the scheme. Duty-free purchases are separate: no VAT was charged, so there is nothing to claim.

    Five mistakes that cost travellers money

    • Splitting one purchase across payments. Two receipts of ₩30,000 return ₩4,000 together; one receipt of ₩60,000 returns ₩3,000 — check the tiers before you split, because it can go either way.
    • Forgetting the passport. Shops cannot add it after the sale.
    • Leaving it too late. The three-month export window starts at purchase, not at the end of your trip.
    • Assuming every Olive Young does it. In-store refunds are only at certified shops; look for the sign, and ask before paying.
    • Queuing at the wrong counter downtown. A Global Blue counter will not process a Global Tax Free receipt.

    Questions travellers ask

    How much of my purchase do I get back?

    About 3% to 8% of the price. The VAT itself is 9.1% of what you paid, and the operator's fee comes out of that. Bigger receipts sit at the higher end.

    What is the minimum purchase?

    ₩15,000 in a single transaction at one store. Two receipts of ₩9,000 do not add up to a qualifying purchase.

    What are the instant refund limits?

    Under ₩1,000,000 per purchase at the till, and no more than ₩5,000,000 across all instant refunds in one trip. Both limits doubled on 1 January 2024.

    Do I need my passport?

    Yes, at the moment you pay. A completed sale cannot be given a refund receipt afterwards.

    How long do I have to leave Korea with the goods?

    Three months from the purchase date, unused and unopened.

    Why is my refund less than 9.1%?

    9.1% is the tax, not the payout. Operators pay fixed amounts per price tier and keep an approved handling fee, so the money you receive is 3–8% of the price.

    Do restaurants and hotels count?

    No. The scheme covers goods you export, not meals, accommodation or transport.

    Is this the same as duty free?

    No. Duty-free shops sell without VAT, so there is nothing to claim. Tax refund shops charge the tax and you claim it back.

    Can I use this for online orders shipped to me?

    No. This calculator covers in-store purchases. Orders exported by a shop follow different export rules, and the shop usually handles them for you.
